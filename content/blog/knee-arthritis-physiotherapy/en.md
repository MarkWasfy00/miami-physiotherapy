Knee osteoarthritis is one of the most common joint problems, especially with age, extra body weight or after old injuries. But arthritis doesn't have to mean living with pain — physiotherapy plays a big role in easing symptoms and keeping you moving.

## What is knee arthritis?

It's the gradual wear of the cartilage that covers the ends of the bones inside the joint, leading to more friction, pain and stiffness.

## Common symptoms

- Knee pain that worsens with walking or climbing stairs.
- Stiffness in the morning or after sitting.
- Clicking or grinding when you move.
- Mild swelling around the joint.
- Difficulty fully bending or straightening the knee.

## How physiotherapy helps

- **Strengthening the muscles around the knee** — strong quadriceps take load off the joint.
- **Improving range of motion** — through manual therapy and flexibility work.
- **Reducing pain and inflammation** — with ultrasound, electrotherapy or shockwave therapy where appropriate.
- **Improving how you walk** — and teaching safer ways to sit and climb stairs.

## Simple exercises that help

Your therapist should choose the right exercises for you. Common examples include:

- Quadriceps sets: tightening the thigh with the knee straight while seated.
- Straight-leg raises lying on your back.
- Slow seated knee bends and straightening.
- Light walking or a stationary bike.

## Everyday tips

- Keep a healthy weight to reduce pressure on the joint.
- Avoid sitting on the floor or squatting for long periods.
- Use the handrail on stairs.
- Don't stop moving altogether — regular movement is good for the joint.
