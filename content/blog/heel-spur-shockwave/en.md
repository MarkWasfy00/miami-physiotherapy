Heel pain with your first steps in the morning, or after sitting for a while, is one of the most common complaints we see. It's usually linked to plantar fasciitis or a heel spur. The good news: most cases improve with conservative care, without injections or surgery.

## What are heel spurs and plantar fasciitis?

The plantar fascia is a strong band of tissue running under your foot from the heel to the toes, supporting the arch. Repeated strain can inflame it and cause heel pain. A heel spur is a small bony growth that may show up on an X-ray — but the pain often comes from inflamed tissue around it rather than the spur itself.

## Common symptoms

- Sharp pain under the heel with your first morning steps.
- Pain that worsens after long periods of standing or walking.
- Tenderness when pressing the middle of the heel.
- Pain that eases with movement, then returns after rest.

## How does shockwave therapy work?

A shockwave device delivers high-energy acoustic pulses to the painful area. This can help stimulate natural healing, improve circulation in the tissue and reduce pain sensitivity. A session takes just a few minutes, and the intensity is adjusted to your comfort.

## How many sessions will I need?

Shockwave sessions are usually weekly over several weeks. The exact number depends on how severe and long-standing the problem is and how you respond. Your therapist will set the plan after your assessment.

## Exercise is essential

Machines alone aren't enough — exercise keeps the results and helps stop the pain coming back:

- Calf stretches against a wall.
- Plantar fascia stretches by pulling the toes back.
- Rolling the sole over a cold water bottle.
- Strengthening the small muscles of the foot.

## Everyday tips

- Wear comfortable shoes with good arch support.
- Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors.
- Reduce excess weight, if relevant, to ease pressure on the foot.
- Limit long periods of standing during flare-ups.
